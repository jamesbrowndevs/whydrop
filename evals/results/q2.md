## Ranked explanation (verified claims only)
### 1. Overall demand is seasonally down across all channels, so paid spend appears weaker but organic and email are declining too.
**Confidence 0.90** - est. impact $4,251/month - Organic and email sessions and revenue declined from August to September 2026, indicating broader seasonal demand weakness alongside the sharper paid social drop.

- Organic web sessions fell 6.16% from August to September 2026 (16,482 to 15,467).  
  _evidence: `Q15` row 1 `aug_sessions`=16482; `Q15` row 1 `sep_sessions`=15467; `Q15` row 1 `pct_change`=-0.06158233224123286_
- Email web sessions fell 4.97% from August to September 2026 (5,669 to 5,387).  
  _evidence: `Q15` row 0 `aug_sessions`=5669; `Q15` row 0 `sep_sessions`=5387; `Q15` row 0 `pct_change`=-0.049744222967013585_
- Organic order revenue fell 20.32% from August to September 2026 ($5,604 to $4,465).  
  _evidence: `Q16` row 1 `aug_rev`=5604.0; `Q16` row 1 `sep_rev`=4465.0; `Q16` row 1 `pct_change`=-0.20324768022840828_

**Recommended action:** Monitor seasonal demand trends and recalibrate paid social spend targets; explore promotional tactics to offset organic and email softness.

### 2. Paid social clicks are not reaching the site because landing_404s spiked, so ad spend is wasted on broken landing pages.
**Confidence 0.93** - est. impact $2,434/month - Paid social landing 404 errors spiked while orders and revenue collapsed, indicating broken landing pages are wasting spend.

- Paid social landing_404s spiked from 44 in 2026-08 to 8408 in 2026-09.  
  _evidence: `Q1` row 0 `total_404s`=44; `Q1` row 1 `total_404s`=8408_
- Paid social orders fell from 196 in 2026-08 to 28 in 2026-09, and revenue fell from 2866.0 to 432.0.  
  _evidence: `Q3` row 0 `total_orders`=196; `Q3` row 1 `total_orders`=28; `Q3` row 0 `total_revenue`=2866.0; `Q3` row 1 `total_revenue`=432.0_

**Recommended action:** Fix or redirect paid social landing pages and pause spend on broken URLs until 404s are resolved.

## Rejected hypotheses (tested, evidence says no)
- **Paid social (meta) spend is no longer efficient: cost per click and cost per order have risen while return on ad spend has fallen.** - ROAS and cost per order deteriorated sharply, but CPC actually fell slightly, so the specific claim that cost per click rose is contradicted by the data.  
  _evidence: `Q9` row 0 `roas`=0.5730521525446436; `Q9` row 1 `roas`=0.09329647546648237; `Q9` row 0 `orders_per_spend`=0.03918988900863578; `Q9` row 1 `orders_per_spend`=0.006046993780234968_
- **A recent price increase or product release reduced conversion of paid social traffic, making ad spend look ineffective even though traffic is intact.** - Paid social sessions collapsed from August to September while conversion rate slightly increased, so the issue is traffic loss rather than conversion suppression.  
  _evidence: `Q4` row 0 `total_sessions`=242285.0; `Q4` row 1 `total_sessions`=32181.0_
- **Refunds and quality complaints on paid social orders are rising, so customers acquired by ads are not retaining value and spend is effectively wasted.** - Paid social refunds fell from $64.00 in August to $12.00 in September and quality-related tickets stayed at zero, so the claim that rising refunds/quality complaints are wasting ad spend is not supported by the data.  
  _evidence: `Q10` row 4 `total_refund`=64.0; `Q10` row 5 `total_refund`=12.0_
- **Paid social is shifting toward lower-converting geographies or lower-priced products, reducing revenue per click even if clicks and sessions hold up.** - Paid social revenue per order and average unit price both rose from August to September while order volume collapsed, so the revenue drop is not explained by a shift to lower-priced products or lower-converting geographies.  
  _evidence: `Q18` row 0 `avg_unit_price_usd`=14.622448979591837; `Q18` row 1 `avg_unit_price_usd`=15.428571428571429; `Q18` row 0 `revenue_per_order`=14.622448979591837; `Q18` row 1 `revenue_per_order`=15.428571428571429_

## Inconclusive / failed verification
- Ad spend is still working but the budget was simply cut, so lower paid volume reflects lower spend rather than lower efficiency. (inconclusive)
